# Learn

Introduces key concepts from multi-agent systems research and explains how each applies to agentic AI.

## Conventions

- One markdown file per topic, prefixed with a number for reading order (e.g. `01-intelligent-agents.md`).
- Each topic covers the classical concept, then its relevance to current agentic AI.

## Contents

1. [Setting the Scene](01-setting-the-scene.md): origins of MAS, the micro/macro problems, intelligent agents and environment properties, team versus self-interested MAS, and how agents and MAS differ from objects, single agents, and related fields (Wooldridge, Vlassis, and Weiss, Chapter 1), and the LLM-era view: agent loop, components, architectures, and protocols (Sayfan, Part 1), and the control-theory view of topology and guarantees (Nagahara, Azuma, and Ahn).
2. [Agent Architectures](02-agent-architectures.md): logic-based, reactive, BDI, and layered architectures, and where LLM agents fit among them (Weiss Ch. 1 section 3).
3. [Agent Landscape 2026](03-agent-landscape-2026.md): dated snapshot of vendor agent products, OpenClaw, and the arrival of open multi-agent systems, mapped to the concepts in 01 and 02.
4. [Multiagent Organisations](04-multiagent-organisations.md): organisations as entities separate from their agents, roles and dependencies, institutions and norms, permission versus power, and organisational change (Weiss Ch. 2, Dignum and Padget).
5. [Trust and Reputation](05-trust-and-reputation.md): soft security, trust as evaluation and decision, reputation and its sources, centralised versus decentralised reputation, and attacks on reputation systems (Weiss Ch. 9, Sabater-Mir and Vercouter).
6. [Control of Cooperative MAS](06-control-of-cooperative-mas.md): consensus, coverage, and formation control, the network conditions for each, and what carries over to LLM agents (Nagahara et al. Ch. 3-5).
7. [Agent Communication](07-agent-communication.md): communication as action, speech acts, ontologies, KQML and FIPA ACL, message flow versus mentalist versus commitment semantics, and MCP and A2A compared with the classical languages (Wooldridge Ch. 6-7, Weiss Ch. 3, Sayfan Ch. 7-8).
8. [Working Together](08-working-together.md): cooperative distributed problem solving, the Contract Net, result sharing, coordination (partial global planning, joint intentions, mutual modelling, social laws), multiagent planning, and Sayfan's orchestration patterns and coordination failures mapped to them (Wooldridge Ch. 8, Sayfan Ch. 8 and 10).
9. [Strategic Interaction](09-strategic-interaction.md): utility, dominant strategies and Nash equilibrium, social dilemmas, repeated play, program equilibria, sequential and Bayesian games, and computation (Wooldridge Ch. 11, Weiss Ch. 17).
10. [Group Decisions](10-group-decisions.md): voting rules, Arrow and Gibbard-Satterthwaite, manipulation and agenda control, decisions over linked issues, and fair division (Wooldridge Ch. 12, Weiss Ch. 6).
11. [Coalitions and Auctions](11-coalitions-and-auctions.md): mechanism design and the revelation principle, the core and Shapley value, coalition structure generation, auctions, combinatorial auctions, and VCG (Wooldridge Ch. 13-14, Weiss Ch. 7-8).
12. [Bargaining and Argumentation](12-bargaining-and-argumentation.md): negotiation settings, Nash and Rubinstein bargaining, multi-issue and heuristic negotiation, task-oriented domains, argumentation-based negotiation, abstract and structured argumentation, dialogues, and strategic argumentation (Wooldridge Ch. 15-16, Weiss Ch. 4-5).
